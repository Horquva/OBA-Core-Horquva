3. Information hierarchy 

-1 Critical Alerts 
-2 Today's KPIs 
-3 Ai Summary 
-4 Department Performance 
-5 Recent Activity 
-6 Notification (lies on top bar )


#1 Critical Alerts (Highest Priority)

Why first?

Because if something urgent is happening, executives need to know immediately.

Examples:

Server failure
Security incident
Revenue dropped 25%
Production stopped

If these were hidden lower on the page, the executive might miss something important.

#2 Today's KPIs

Why second?

Executives usually open dashboards to answer questions like:

How are we performing today?
Are we on target?
Is the business healthy?

KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) give that answer in a few seconds.

Examples:

Revenue
Profit
Active Projects
Employee Attendance


#3 AI Summary

Why after KPIs?

Numbers tell you what happened.

AI tells you why it happened.

For example:

KPI:

Revenue decreased by 8%.

AI Summary:

Revenue decreased because sales in the North region dropped while operating costs increased.

That explanation becomes much more useful after seeing the numbers.

#4 Department Performance

Once executives understand the overall company, they naturally want to drill down.

Questions become:

Which department is causing the issue?
Which team is performing best?

So department-level information comes next.

#5 Recent Activity

Executives often need context.

Examples:

New employee joined.
Budget approved.
New project started.
Marketing campaign launched.

This is useful information, but it's generally less urgent than company performance.


#6 Notifications

Why in top bar ?

Notifications are usually supporting information.

Examples:

Someone commented on a report.
Meeting reminder.
Software update.

They're important, but they usually shouldn't distract from the organization's overall health.
