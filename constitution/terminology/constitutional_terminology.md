# Horquva Constitutional Terminology Dictionary

> Version: 1.0
>
> Purpose: Establish a single, consistent vocabulary for all engineers working on the Horquva Organizational Brain Architecture (OBA). These definitions align with the frozen constitutional architecture and should be used consistently throughout documentation, design discussions, and implementation.

---

# Introduction

The Organizational Brain (OBA) is built on a constitutional architecture where every platform has a clearly defined responsibility. Consistent terminology ensures that engineers, architects, and stakeholders describe the system using the same language.

These definitions represent architectural concepts rather than implementation details.

---

# Core Concepts

## Organizational Brain (OBA)

The Organizational Brain is the continuously operating intelligence system that observes, understands, remembers, reasons about, and assists an organization. It integrates multiple constitutional intelligence modules into a unified cognitive platform for enterprise decision support.

---

## Organizational Cognitive Operating System (OCOS)

The long-term evolution of the Organizational Brain into a continuously running cognitive operating system that maintains an up-to-date understanding of organizational reality and supports executive decision-making.

---

## Constitutional Architecture

The frozen architectural blueprint that defines how information flows through the Organizational Brain. All engineering work must align with this architecture.

---

## Constitutional Intelligence Modules

The 55 specialized intelligence modules responsible for analyzing organizational reality from different perspectives (such as Risk, Ownership, Learning, Simulation, Governance, and Executive Intelligence). Each module produces evidence-based intelligence rather than interacting directly with executives.

---

## Meta-Brain

The orchestration layer that combines outputs from multiple constitutional intelligence modules into a unified executive response.

---

# Cognitive Pipeline

## Enterprise Reality

The real-world organization being observed, including people, systems, workflows, projects, tools, documents, events, and business operations.

---

## Connectivity

The constitutional layer responsible for connecting to enterprise systems and collecting raw organizational data without interpreting it.

---

## Perception

The process of observing enterprise reality and converting raw organizational data into standardized observations for downstream processing.

---

## Evidence

Validated and trustworthy organizational observations that support reasoning and decision-making. Every recommendation should be backed by evidence.

---

## Understanding

The process of interpreting validated evidence to determine its organizational meaning and context.

---

## Knowledge

Structured organizational facts, relationships, and insights that describe how the organization operates.

---

## Organizational Memory

The persistent storage of organizational experience, historical context, and accumulated knowledge over time.

---

## World Model

The continuously updated internal representation of the organization. It models organizational entities, relationships, dependencies, ownership, workflows, and current state.

All constitutional components either contribute to or consume the World Model.

---

## Runtime

The execution environment responsible for coordinating platform operations, module execution, orchestration, and lifecycle management.

---

## Reasoning

The cognitive process of analyzing evidence, knowledge, and the World Model to generate conclusions, predictions, recommendations, and decisions.

---

## Scientific Intelligence

The constitutional layer responsible for producing evidence-based beliefs, predictions, simulations, confidence scores, and recommendations using outputs from the intelligence modules.

---

## Executive Cognitive Layer

The layer that transforms organizational intelligence into executive decision intelligence suitable for leadership.

---

## Executive Experience

The user-facing layer that delivers intelligence through dashboards, conversations, briefings, visualizations, reports, simulations, and voice interfaces.

---

## Enterprise Actions

Approved workflows, automations, or operational changes executed as a result of organizational intelligence. These actions modify enterprise reality and begin a new cognitive cycle.

---

# Intelligence Concepts

## Signal

An observed organizational indicator that may represent an event, trend, anomaly, opportunity, or risk requiring further analysis.

---

## Entity

A uniquely identifiable organizational object represented within the World Model, such as a person, department, workflow, AI agent, document, project, or software system.

---

## Relationship

A defined connection between two or more entities that describes organizational structure, ownership, dependency, collaboration, or interaction.

---

## Dependency

A relationship where one entity relies on another for successful operation or execution.

---

## Ownership

The assignment of responsibility for an organizational asset, workflow, system, or intelligence object.

---

## Organizational State

The current representation of organizational conditions stored within the World Model, including active entities, relationships, health indicators, and operational status.

---

## Organizational Health

A measurement of the organization's operational condition derived from multiple intelligence modules and organizational signals.

---

## Organizational Intelligence

Evidence-based insights generated by constitutional intelligence modules to improve organizational understanding and executive decision-making.

---

# Cognitive Objects

## Observation

Raw information collected from enterprise reality before validation.

---

## Evidence Object

A validated organizational observation that can support reasoning and recommendations.

---

## Knowledge Object

A structured representation of organizational information stored within the knowledge layer.

---

## Memory Object

A persistent historical record stored within Organizational Memory.

---

## Belief

An evidence-supported conclusion about the organization. Every belief should be accompanied by supporting evidence and a confidence score.

---

## Confidence

A quantitative measure expressing how strongly available evidence supports a belief, recommendation, prediction, or conclusion.

---

## Recommendation

An evidence-based suggested action generated by one or more intelligence modules to improve organizational outcomes.

---

## Prediction

An estimate of a possible future organizational outcome based on current knowledge, evidence, and reasoning.

---

## Simulation

The evaluation of hypothetical organizational scenarios to understand potential future impacts before actions are taken.

---

## Executive Briefing

A concise summary of the organization's most important intelligence, risks, opportunities, and recommended actions presented to executives.

---

# Supporting Components

## Knowledge Graph

A structured graph representing organizational entities and their relationships, enabling discovery, dependency analysis, and reasoning.

---

## Module Registry

The catalog containing all constitutional intelligence modules and their capabilities.

---

## Capability Registry

The registry describing which intelligence capabilities are provided by each constitutional module.

---

## Execution Engine

The runtime component responsible for coordinating and executing constitutional intelligence modules.

---

## Connector

A component responsible for collecting data from external enterprise systems without performing interpretation or reasoning.

---

## Governance

The constitutional mechanisms ensuring that recommendations, reasoning, and actions remain controlled, auditable, explainable, and compliant.

---

# Engineering Principles

The following principles apply across the entire platform:

- Architecture precedes implementation.
- Every platform has a single constitutional responsibility.
- Connectors observe reality but do not interpret it.
- Evidence validates observations before reasoning.
- Knowledge stores organizational facts.
- Memory preserves organizational history.
- The World Model represents current organizational reality.
- Intelligence modules reason about the World Model.
- Recommendations must be supported by evidence.
- Beliefs must include confidence.
- Actions require governance.
- No component should bypass the constitutional cognitive pipeline.
