## Day 1 — Enterprise Structure Design

### Purpose

This section defines the constitutional organizational structure that every synthetic enterprise generated by Arcturus is built from — a hierarchy flexible enough to represent a 10-person startup or a 50,000-person institution, while staying consistent enough for downstream platforms to build on.

### Component Definitions

| Layer | Type | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Organization** | Core | The synthetic enterprise itself — the root entity. |
| **Executive Office** | Core | Top governing layer: CEO/CTO/CFO/COO and direct staff. Holds ultimate decision authority. |
| **Business Unit** | Optional | Semi-independent segment of the company, often with its own budget/P&L. |
| **Division** | Optional | Grouping within a Business Unit, typically by geography or product line. |
| **Department** | Core | Functional group responsible for a specific business capability. |
| **Team** | Core | Small working group inside a department with a focused scope of work. |
| **Shared Services** | Optional | Centralized functions serving the whole organization (IT, Legal, Facilities). |
| **Functional Area** | Core | Broad category grouping related departments for reporting/analysis. |
| **Leadership** | Core | Individuals holding decision authority at any layer (Directors, VPs, Managers, Leads). |
| **Reporting Hierarchy** | Core | Chain-of-command relationships connecting every role to the Executive Office. |

### Hierarchical Enterprise Diagram

The diagram below illustrates how every component defined above relates to the others, including where Leadership and Reporting Hierarchy sit relative to the structural layers.

```
ORGANIZATION
   |
   +-- EXECUTIVE OFFICE
   |     (Leadership: CEO / CFO / COO — top of the Reporting Hierarchy)
   |
   +-- BUSINESS UNIT (optional)
   |     |
   |     +-- DIVISION (optional)
   |           |
   |           +-- FUNCTIONAL AREA
   |                 |
   |                 +-- DEPARTMENT   (Leadership: Director)
   |                       |
   |                       +-- TEAM   (Leadership: Manager / Team Lead)
   |                              -- reports up the Reporting Hierarchy
   |                                 to the Executive Office
   |
   +-- SHARED SERVICES (optional, parallel to Business Units)
         |
         +-- IT   +-- Legal   +-- Facilities
```

### Scaling Rule

- **Small org (≤ ~50 people):** Organization → Executive Office → Department → Team only.
- **Mid-size org (~50–500 people):** adds Shared Services once departments would otherwise duplicate effort.
- **Large org (500+ people):** adds Business Units and Divisions to segment by product line, geography, or market.

### Relationship Rules

- Every entity below Organization has exactly one parent (strict tree, no dual-reporting in v1.0).
- Executive Office always sits directly under Organization — never under a Business Unit.
- Shared Services sits parallel to Business Units, reporting to the Executive Office.
- Team is always the lowest structural layer — no sub-teams in v1.0.

## Day 2 — Business Functions & Operating Models

This section defines the responsibilities and relationships of each of the 15 constitutional business functions used across every synthetic enterprise, regardless of industry. Each function lists its responsibilities, what it depends on, what it provides to other functions, and its escalation path.

### Executive Management
- **Mission:** Set strategic direction and make final accountability decisions for the organization.
- **Responsibilities:** Strategic planning, resource allocation, cross-functional prioritization, final governance sign-off.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** All functions (for reporting/status)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** All functions (direction, approval authority)
- **Escalation Path:** Terminal — top of the chain

### Human Resources (HR)
- **Mission:** Manage the organization's people lifecycle and workforce policy.
- **Responsibilities:** Hiring, onboarding, performance management, compensation, workforce policy, employee relations.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Finance (budget/headcount approval), Legal (employment compliance)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Every department (staffing, policy)
- **Escalation Path:** HR Director → Executive Management

### Finance
- **Mission:** Manage the organization's financial health and resource allocation.
- **Responsibilities:** Budgeting, forecasting, accounting, financial reporting, capital allocation, expense approval.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Operations (cost data), Sales (revenue data)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Executive Management (reporting), every department (budget approval)
- **Escalation Path:** CFO → Executive Management

### Operations
- **Mission:** Ensure the day-to-day functioning and delivery capability of the organization.
- **Responsibilities:** Process management, resource scheduling, vendor coordination, operational continuity.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Procurement (supplies/vendors), IT (systems), Finance (budget)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Sales/Customer Success (delivery capacity), Executive Management (status)
- **Escalation Path:** COO → Executive Management

### Sales
- **Mission:** Generate revenue by acquiring and expanding customer relationships.
- **Responsibilities:** Lead qualification, deal negotiation, contract closing, pipeline management.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Marketing (leads), Legal (contract review), Product (capability info)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Finance (revenue data), Customer Success (new accounts)
- **Escalation Path:** VP Sales → Executive Management

### Marketing
- **Mission:** Build market awareness and generate demand for the organization's offerings.
- **Responsibilities:** Brand strategy, campaigns, content, lead generation, market positioning.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Product (positioning input), R&D (innovation narrative)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Sales (qualified leads), Executive Management (market insight)
- **Escalation Path:** VP Marketing → Executive Management

### Customer Success
- **Mission:** Ensure customers realize ongoing value and remain retained.
- **Responsibilities:** Onboarding, account health monitoring, renewal management, escalation handling.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Sales (handoff), Product (feature knowledge), IT (issue resolution)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Sales (renewal/expansion), Executive Management (retention metrics)
- **Escalation Path:** Director of Customer Success → Executive Management

### Engineering
- **Mission:** Build and maintain the organization's technical products and systems.
- **Responsibilities:** Software/systems development, technical architecture, reliability, technical debt management.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Product (requirements), IT (infrastructure), Security (design standards)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Product (delivered capability), Customer Success (technical support)
- **Escalation Path:** VP Engineering → Executive Management

### Product
- **Mission:** Define what the organization builds and why.
- **Responsibilities:** Product strategy, roadmap planning, requirements definition, feature prioritization.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Marketing/Sales (market feedback), R&D (emerging capability)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Engineering (requirements), Marketing (positioning input)
- **Escalation Path:** VP Product → Executive Management

### Legal
- **Mission:** Protect the organization from legal risk and ensure contractual soundness.
- **Responsibilities:** Contract review, regulatory interpretation, dispute management, IP protection.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Compliance (regulatory status), Sales (contract terms)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Every function (legal risk guidance), Executive Management (risk reporting)
- **Escalation Path:** General Counsel → Executive Management

### Compliance
- **Mission:** Ensure the organization operates within regulatory and policy boundaries.
- **Responsibilities:** Regulatory monitoring, audit management, policy enforcement, reporting to regulators.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Legal (regulatory interpretation), Operations (process adherence data)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Legal (compliance status), Executive Management (risk exposure)
- **Escalation Path:** Chief Compliance Officer → Executive Management

### Procurement
- **Mission:** Acquire the goods, services, and vendor relationships the organization needs.
- **Responsibilities:** Vendor sourcing, contract negotiation, purchase order management, supplier relationship management.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Finance (budget approval), Legal (vendor contract review)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Operations (supplies), IT (equipment/licenses)
- **Escalation Path:** Director of Procurement → Operations → Executive Management

### Information Technology (IT)
- **Mission:** Provide and maintain the technology infrastructure the organization runs on.
- **Responsibilities:** Systems administration, infrastructure, technical support, software provisioning.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Security (security standards), Procurement (hardware/software acquisition)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Every function (systems access and support)
- **Escalation Path:** IT Director → Operations → Executive Management

### Security
- **Mission:** Protect the organization's people, data, and systems from threats.
- **Responsibilities:** Access control, threat monitoring, incident response, security policy enforcement.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** IT (system visibility), Compliance (regulatory security requirements)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Every function (secure environment), Engineering (secure design standards)
- **Escalation Path:** CISO → Executive Management

### Research & Development (R&D)
- **Mission:** Explore new capabilities, technologies, and innovations for the organization's future.
- **Responsibilities:** Applied research, prototyping, feasibility studies, innovation pipeline management.
- **Relationships — Depends On:** Product (strategic direction), Engineering (technical feasibility input)
- **Relationships — Provides To:** Product (innovation input), Marketing (differentiation narrative)
- **Escalation Path:** VP R&D → Executive Management

## Day 3 — Enterprise Templates & Governance Models

This section defines 8 reusable enterprise templates, each built from the Day 1 hierarchy and Day 2 business functions. For each template, the required fields are: Organization Size, Department Structure, Leadership Model, Governance Structure, Business Functions, and Organizational Objectives.

### Startup

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 5–50 people |
| **Department Structure** | Minimum hierarchy only: Organization → Executive Office → Department → Team. No Business Units, Divisions, or Shared Services. |
| **Leadership Model** | Flat — founder(s)/CEO directly oversee all department leads. |
| **Governance Structure** | Founder-led; informal governance; investors may hold advisory board seats. |
| **Business Functions** | Executive Management, Engineering, Product, Sales, Marketing, Operations (lightweight). HR/Finance/Legal often outsourced. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Speed of iteration, product-market fit, growth, runway management. |

### Enterprise SaaS Company

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 500–10,000 people |
| **Department Structure** | Full hierarchy: Organization → Executive Office → Business Unit (Product Lines) → Department → Team. Shared Services active. |
| **Leadership Model** | Layered — CEO → VPs per function → Directors → Managers → Team Leads. |
| **Governance Structure** | Executive Committee + Board of Directors; formal quarterly governance reviews. |
| **Business Functions** | All 15 functions active, fully staffed. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Recurring revenue growth, customer retention, platform scalability, market share. |

### Manufacturing Company

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 1,000–20,000 people |
| **Department Structure** | Full hierarchy including Divisions by product line/plant location. Centralized Shared Services (IT, Legal, Procurement). |
| **Leadership Model** | Plant-level leadership reporting into regional/divisional VPs, up to Executive Office. |
| **Governance Structure** | Board of Directors; strong Compliance/Safety oversight; union relations under HR. |
| **Business Functions** | Operations and Procurement dominant; Engineering focused on process engineering; heavy Compliance (safety, environmental). |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Production efficiency, supply chain reliability, quality/safety compliance, cost control. |

### Hospital

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 500–15,000 people |
| **Department Structure** | Business Units by clinical service line, Departments as clinical/admin units, Shared Services for IT/Facilities/Procurement. |
| **Leadership Model** | Dual track — Clinical leadership (CMO, Department Chiefs) parallel to Administrative leadership (CEO, COO, CFO). |
| **Governance Structure** | Hospital Board + Medical Executive Committee; heavy regulatory/accreditation oversight. |
| **Business Functions** | Compliance & Legal heavily weighted (patient safety); Operations includes clinical operations; Customer Success → Patient Experience; Security includes patient data protection. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Patient outcomes, regulatory compliance, operational capacity, cost of care. |

### University

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 500–20,000 people |
| **Department Structure** | Business Units as Colleges/Faculties, Departments as academic departments, Shared Services for IT/Facilities/HR/Procurement. |
| **Leadership Model** | Dual track — Academic leadership (Provost, Deans, Chairs) parallel to Administrative leadership (President, CFO, COO). |
| **Governance Structure** | Board of Trustees/Regents; Academic Senate; accreditation body oversight. |
| **Business Functions** | R&D prominent (research grants); Sales/Marketing → Admissions & Advancement; Customer Success → Student Services; Compliance covers accreditation/research ethics. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Academic reputation/rankings, research output, student enrollment/retention, endowment growth. |

### Government Agency

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 100–50,000+ people |
| **Department Structure** | Business Units as Bureaus/Directorates, Departments as functional units, centralized Shared Services per policy. |
| **Leadership Model** | Politically appointed Executive (Secretary/Director) at top; career civil-service leadership runs day-to-day operations. |
| **Governance Structure** | Legislative oversight, public accountability/audit requirements, inspector general function. |
| **Business Functions** | Compliance & Legal dominant; Sales/Marketing largely absent or → Public Communications; Procurement heavily formalized; Security covers cyber and physical. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Public service delivery, regulatory/legal compliance, budget stewardship, public trust. |

### Financial Institution

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 1,000–100,000+ people |
| **Department Structure** | Business Units by product line (Retail/Investment Banking, Wealth Mgmt), Divisions by region, centralized Shared Services. |
| **Leadership Model** | Highly layered — CEO → Business Unit Heads → Regional Directors → Branch/Team Managers. |
| **Governance Structure** | Board of Directors + Risk Committee + Audit Committee; heavy regulatory oversight (central bank/financial regulators). |
| **Business Functions** | Compliance & Security extremely dominant; Legal heavily staffed; Risk Management alongside Compliance; Sales → Relationship Management/Advisory. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Risk management, regulatory compliance, asset growth, customer trust, profitability. |

### Retail Enterprise

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Organization Size** | 500–100,000+ people |
| **Department Structure** | Business Units by region/store format, Departments for corporate functions, Teams at store/warehouse level. Shared Services for IT/Procurement/HR. |
| **Leadership Model** | Corporate leadership (CEO, VPs) overseeing Regional Directors, who oversee Store/District Managers. |
| **Governance Structure** | Board of Directors; Executive Committee; consumer protection compliance applies. |
| **Business Functions** | Procurement & Operations dominant (supply chain, inventory, logistics); Sales/Marketing heavily weighted; Customer Success → Customer Experience. |
| **Organizational Objectives** | Sales growth, inventory/supply chain efficiency, customer experience, margin optimization. |
